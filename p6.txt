Abstract:
The growing use of Large Language Models (LLMs) on edge devices presents serious challenges in balancing latency, computational performance, and ecological sustainability. Conventional methods focus mainly on energy efficiency in datacenters and neglect the specific constraints and real-time dynamics of distributed edge environments. The paper here introduces a Carbon-Aware LLM Inference Framework for Edge Devices, one that adjusts model behavior dynamically according to real-time carbon intensity signals from APIs like WattTime and ElectricityMap. The framework consists of four functional layers, Sensing & Signal Collection, Device & Model Profiling, Adaptive Inference Controller, and Execution & Accounting, that facilitate per-request inference decisions with the aid of approximation methods like quantization, early exits, and distillation. Experiments over benchmark tasks show that the system proposed attains between 80% CO2 emission savings and drastic latency reductions (from 250 ms to 160), while maintaining the proposed system accuracy 92.7%. Trade-offs between multiple objectives enable robust tuning between sustainability, accuracy, and responsiveness, enabling deployment on a wide range of real-world use cases. This paper fills the gap between green AI research and real-world edge intelligence, presenting a scalable, eco-friendly method for LLM inference.

I. Introduction
Large Language Models (LLMs), such as GPT, LLaMA, and PaLM, have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in natural language understanding, generation, and reasoning [1]. Their popularity across applications that include healthcare and education, as well as business analytics, has driven record-high growth in artificial intelligence (AI) [2]. While the pace of development has brought enormous value to users, it also comes with substantial challenges, predominantly the large computational and energy requirements of LLMs [3]. Recent work highlights that the training and inference processes in large-scale AI models contribute significantly to carbon emissions, raising questions about the environmental sustainability of AI systems [4]. To overcome these challenges, scientists have begun investigating energy-efficient AI and carbon-conscious computing approaches [5]. Current studies mainly look at datacenter-scale approaches like carbon-conscious workload scheduling, integration of renewable energy, and energy-efficient model training. Although these are significant approaches, they often overlook the growing trend of edge intelligence, where LLMs are being increasingly deployed on distributed and resource-limited edge devices [6]. This edge movement is motivated by low latency, data privacy, and realtime decision-making needs in smart cities, autonomous vehicles, and personalized healthcare [7]. Deploying LLMs on the edge, however, increases performance compared to sustainability tension. Edge devices possess limited computation capacities and tend to draw power from local energy grids with variable carbon intensities [8]. Real-time carbon signals are very seldom considered in current work on how to guide and optimize LLM inference within such devices. This is an urgent opportunity, supporting carbonconscious adaptive inference algorithms that trade off against accuracy, efficiency, and environmental sustainability.
