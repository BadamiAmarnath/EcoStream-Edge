Abstract
The rapid rise of cloud computing and edge services has dramatically increased the carbon footprint associated with digital infrastructure, leading to calls for new solutions to aid in the sustainable management of resources. This paper proposes a unique carbon-aware workload orchestrator for federated edge-cloud systems that balances carbon emissions, energy costs, service-level agreement (SLA) violations, and resource utilization with a dynamic spatio-temporal workload management approach. The proposed approach incorporates adaptive weighting based on real-time carbon intensity, federation constraints, and multi-domain resource optimization, and address critical gaps in existing carbon-aware scheduling methods. We show results of a detailed simulation with 3969 unique workloads over 7 days across 3 cloud data centers and 9 edge nodes, where the proposed algorithm showed statistically significant improvement over static scheduling baselines, including a 10.2% reduction in carbon emissions (

The orchestrator uses intelligent decision-making that modifies operation according to real-time and local environmental conditions while determining the level of operational efficiency across federated domains. An additional analysis of the framework’s ability to guarantee fairness for each workload type has been identified through a head-to-head competition against state-of-the-art approaches, showing improvement across the majority of metrics, including significant improvement in fairness (21.6%) and eliminating SLA violations for the emergency workload type. The work shows the critical importance of combined carbon awareness in resource provisioning for sustainable cloud–edge computing and presents an approach for environmentally conscious workload provisioning across distributed systems.
Introduction
The fast growth of cloud computing and edge services has made digital infrastructure an important factor in global carbon emissions, with data centers contributing to approximately 1%–2% of global electricity consumption [1]. According to the International Energy Agency, the worldwide electricity demand in data centers has risen to around 460 TWh in 2022, up from 300 TWh in 2019, with artificial intelligence applications leading to this increase, and it is expected to keep increasing to a range of 620–1050 TWh in 2026 [2]. The growing need for computing power, coupled with the growing portion of carbon intensity in the generation of electricity in most regions of the world, brings a pressing requirement for sustainable computing models where digital demand and environmental stewardship can be balanced.
Federated edge cloud systems development will create a carbon consumption barrier and benefits to optimized carbon consumption. These distributed systems also cut across various geographical locations, having various patterns of carbon intensity, heterogeneous capabilities of resources, and varying operational constraints [3]. While this distribution makes resource management more complex, it offers novel opportunities for carbon-aware orchestration of workloads through spatial and temporal shifting of computing tasks. Nevertheless, existing scheduling mechanisms generally do not consider these opportunities due to fundamental limitations. Most existing approaches focus only on spatial shifting or temporal shifting, with a handful of approaches considering both [4]. Further, the impact of embodied emissions from hardware production is often overlooked in scheduling decisions, even though this is increasingly likely to be an important consideration as the operation of electricity becomes more decarbonized [5].
Recent developments have progressed various aspects of carbon-aware computing. Alex et al. [6] presented a novel method named CARBON-DQN, which uses deep Q-networks and agglomerative clustering to enable dynamic virtual machine placement, achieving a marginal carbon efficiency improvement of 20%–70%. Siddique [7] created an AI-centered framework to optimize the scheduling of tasks onto servers in a cloud environment while accounting for the carbon intensity of the energy consumed. This framework predicted carbon intensity using machine learning and reinforced learning-based scheduling, reporting carbon emission reductions of up to 42% in their experiments. Asadov et al. [4] carried out a summary of recent carbon-aware scheduling methods, discussing work in spatio-temporal workload shifting whilst considering embodied emissions. However, the majority of the work conducted has been focused on a centralized cloud approach and provided limited treatment of edge-cloud federated constraints and trade-offs of carbon reduction, along with performance guarantees and operational costs in cross-domain settings.
Based on the above analysis of the literature, we identify three critical research gaps that remain unaddressed in existing carbon-aware scheduling approaches for edge-cloud systems:
1.
(G1) Lack of integrated spatio-temporal optimization: Most approaches either treat spatial shifting (e.g., CARBON-DQN [6], CASPER [8]) or temporal shifting (e.g., “Let’s Wait Awhile” [9]) separately, neglecting the advantages of co-optimization of workload assignment between space and time.
2.
(G2) Absence of federation-aware constraints: Prior works (e.g., Siddique [7], Asadov et al. [4]) assume a single administrative domain, ignoring data sovereignty, domain trust boundaries, and cross-domain policy compliance that are essential for real-world federated edge-cloud infrastructures.
3.
(G3) Static or single-objective weighting: Existing multi- objective schedulers use fixed weights or optimize a single metric (e.g., carbon only), failing to adapt to dynamic changes in carbon intensity, workload criticality, or operational costs.
This paper directly addresses these gaps through the following contributions to advance the field of carbon-aware computing in federated edge-cloud systems:
1.
A dynamic adaptive weighting mechanism (addresses G3) that takes advantage of real-time conditions of the environment and criticality of the workload (Section 4.2).
2.
Multi-objective optimization (addresses G1 and G3) based on carbon, cost, performance, and SLA with federation constraints (Sections 3 System model and problem formulation, 4.3 Multi-objective scoring function).
3.
Extensive and formal validation (addresses all gaps) showing statistically significant improvements in performance without undermining cost, SLA, or carbon across different metrics compared to the state-of-the-art (Section 5).
4.
Practical feasibility in heterogeneous federated environments (addresses G2) under realistic constraints (Sections 3 System model and problem formulation, 4.4 Federation-aware placement algorithm).
The remainder of this paper is structured as follows: Section 2 reviews related work on spatial, temporal, hybrid, and recent AI-driven carbon-aware scheduling approaches, as well as federated learning and lifecycle-aware methods. Section 3 presents the system model and problem formulation. Section 4 describes the proposed carbon-aware federated orchestrator, including its architecture, adaptive weighting strategy, multi-objective scoring function, and federation-aware placement algorithm. Section 5 provides a comprehensive performance evaluation, including experimental design, metrics, results, and analysis. Section 6 concludes the paper and outlines directions for future work.
